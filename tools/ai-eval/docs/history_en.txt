# 1 · The French Revolution (1789–1799)
## 1.1 Causes
France faced a severe financial crisis in the 1780s, made worse by debts from the Seven Years' War and from support for the American Revolution.
Bread prices rose sharply after poor harvests in 1788, so many urban workers could not afford food.
Enlightenment writers such as Rousseau and Montesquieu questioned absolute monarchy and the privileges of the nobility.
The tax burden fell mainly on the Third Estate; the clergy and the nobility paid little or no direct tax.

## 1.2 The three Estates
| Estate | Members | Share of population
| First Estate | Clergy | about 0.5 %
| Second Estate | Nobility | about 1.5 %
| Third Estate | Commoners: peasants, workers, bourgeoisie | about 98 %

!! Voting in the Estates-General
Each Estate had one collective vote, so the two privileged Estates could outvote the Third Estate.
The Third Estate demanded voting by head instead of voting by order, but the king did not accept this at first.

# 2 · Key events
## 2.1 The year 1789
Estates-General: opened at Versailles on 5 May 1789.
Tennis Court Oath: 20 June 1789, the National Assembly swore not to separate until France had a constitution.
Storming of the Bastille: 14 July 1789, a symbol of royal tyranny fell to the people of Paris.
Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen: adopted on 26 August 1789.

## 2.2 From monarchy to republic
The revolution did not abolish the monarchy immediately; a constitutional monarchy was created in 1791.
The monarchy was abolished in September 1792 and France became a republic.
Louis XVI was executed in January 1793.

## 2.3 The Reign of Terror
The Committee of Public Safety, led by Robespierre, ruled during the Terror (1793–1794).
Thousands of suspected enemies of the revolution were executed by guillotine.
Robespierre himself was arrested and executed in July 1794, which ended the Terror.

# 3 · Results
The revolution ended feudal privileges and spread the ideas of liberty, equality and fraternity.
Napoleon Bonaparte took power in the coup of 18 Brumaire in November 1799.
The Napoleonic Code of 1804 kept equality before the law but did not give women equal rights.
