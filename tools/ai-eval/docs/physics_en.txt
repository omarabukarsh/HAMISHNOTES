# Unit 2 · Forces and Motion
## 2.1 Newton's laws of motion
First law (inertia): an object stays at rest or moves at constant velocity unless a net external force acts on it.
Second law: the net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration, F = m × a.
Third law: when one object exerts a force on a second object, the second exerts an equal and opposite force on the first.
Action and reaction forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel each other.

## 2.2 SI units
| Quantity | SI unit | Symbol
| Force | newton | N
| Mass | kilogram | kg
| Acceleration | metre per second squared | m/s²
| Momentum | kilogram metre per second | kg·m/s

## 2.3 Weight and mass
Mass is the amount of matter in an object and does not change from place to place.
Weight is the gravitational force on an object, W = m × g, where g ≈ 9.8 m/s² near the Earth's surface.
An astronaut has the same mass on the Moon but a smaller weight, because gravity on the Moon is about one sixth of that on Earth.

!! Friction
Friction always acts opposite to the direction of motion or attempted motion.
Static friction is usually larger than kinetic friction for the same pair of surfaces.

# Unit 3 · Momentum
## 3.1 Conservation of momentum
Momentum is the product of mass and velocity, p = m × v.
In a closed system with no external force, the total momentum before a collision equals the total momentum after it.
In an elastic collision kinetic energy is conserved; in an inelastic collision it is not conserved.
