# 1.2 Mechanism of action
- All β-lactams interfere with bacterial cell-wall synthesis.
- The wall = peptidoglycan strands of alternating NAG (N-acetyl-glucosamine) and NAM (N-acetyl-muramic acid).
- NAM carries short peptide chains that are cross-linked — the last step, done by the enzyme transpeptidase.
- Penicillins (and cephalosporins) bind to and inhibit transpeptidase → no cross-linking → no wall formation.
- PBPs (Penicillin-Binding Proteins) = bacterial membrane enzymes involved in wall synthesis; penicillins inactivate them → morphological changes or lysis.
- Bactericidal only if active wall synthesis occurs — **active against growing, not resting cells**.

# 1.2 Mechanism of action
- All β-lactams interfere with bacterial cell-wall synthesis.
- The wall = peptidoglycan strands of alternating NAG (N-acetyl-glucosamine) and NAM (N-acetyl-muramic acid).
- NAM carries short peptide chains that are cross-linked — the last step, done by the enzyme transpeptidase.
- Penicillins (and cephalosporins) bind to and inhibit transpeptidase.
- This leads to no cross-linking and no wall formation.
- PBPs (Penicillin-Binding Proteins) = bacterial membrane enzymes involved in wall synthesis.
- Penicillins inactivate them → morphological changes or lysis.
- Bactericidal only if active wall synthesis occurs — **active against growing, not resting cells**.
